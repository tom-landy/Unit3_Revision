- Adjustments for prepayments, accruals.

- Purpose and use.

  - non-current assets (tangible and intangible, cost, depreciation and amortisation,
net book value)
  - current assets (inventories, trade receivables, prepayments, bank, cash)
  - current liabilities (bank overdraft, accruals, trade payables)
  - net current assets/liabilities
  - non-current liabilities (bank loan and mortgage)
  - net assets
  - total equity (opening capital, transfer of profit or loss, drawings, closing capital).
- Adjustments for straight line (cost x%) depreciation, reducing balance
(cost – depreciation to date x%).
- Adjustments for prepayments, accruals.
- Interpretation, analysis, and evaluation of statements.

=== KEY POINTS: F1 Statement of comprehensive income (Section B: Business finance) ===
Unit 3 only covers sole traders.

Purpose: the statement of comprehensive income (SOCI) shows whether a business made a profit or loss over a period, usually a year. It's used by the owner (to judge performance and decide drawings), HMRC (tax on profit), lenders and potential buyers.

Layout
Revenue (sales)
Less cost of goods sold (cost of sales):
  Opening inventory
  + Purchases
  − Closing inventory
= Gross profit
+ Other income (rent received, commission received, interest received, discount received)
− Expenses (wages, rent, rates, utilities, insurance, telephone, marketing, depreciation, interest paid, discount allowed, etc.)
= Profit (or loss) for the year

Formulas (learn these)
- Cost of goods sold = opening inventory + purchases − closing inventory
- Gross profit = revenue − cost of goods sold
- Profit for the year = gross profit + other income − expenses

Adjustments
- Depreciation: the year's charge (straight-line: cost × %; reducing balance: (cost − depreciation to date) × %) is added as an expense.
- Accrual: an expense used this year but not yet paid (e.g. an electricity bill still owing). ADD it to the expense. It's also a current liability in the SOFP.
- Prepayment: an expense paid this year that belongs to next year (e.g. rent paid in advance). SUBTRACT it from the expense. It's also a current asset in the SOFP.

Worked example (Sam's Sports)
- Cost of goods sold: £4,000 + £25,000 − £5,000 = £24,000
- Gross profit: £60,000 − £24,000 = £36,000
- Rent: £9,000 − £1,000 prepaid = £8,000
- Electricity: £2,400 + £300 accrued = £2,700
- Depreciation: £15,000 × 10% = £1,500
- Expenses: £8,000 + £2,700 + £14,000 + £1,500 = £26,200
- Profit: £36,000 + £800 commission − £26,200 = £10,600

Interpreting: compare with previous years and competitors. Did revenue rise? Did cost of sales rise faster than revenue (squeezing gross profit)? Which expenses grew? Use ratios (F3) to support the analysis.

Losses are shown in brackets, e.g. (26,310).

=== GLOSSARY: F1 Statement of comprehensive income ===
- Cost of goods sold: Opening inventory + purchases − closing inventory.
- Gross profit: Revenue − cost of goods sold.
- Profit for the year: Gross profit + other income − expenses.
- Accrual: Expense owed but not paid. Add to the expense; current liability.
- Prepayment: Expense paid in advance. Subtract from the expense; current asset.
- Other income: Rent, commission, interest and discount received.
- Purpose of the SOCI: Shows profit or loss over a period.

=== KEY POINTS: F2 Statement of financial position (Section B: Business finance) ===
Purpose: the statement of financial position (SOFP) is a snapshot of what a business owns (assets) and owes (liabilities) on one date, and how it's financed. It shows the net worth of the business. Used by the owner, lenders and potential buyers.

Vertical layout
Non-current assets (cost − accumulated depreciation = net book value)
  Tangible: land, buildings, machinery, vehicles, fixtures and fittings
  Intangible: goodwill, patents, trademarks (amortisation instead of depreciation)
Current assets: inventories, trade receivables, prepayments, bank, cash
Current liabilities (due within a year): bank overdraft, accruals, trade payables
Net current assets (or liabilities) = current assets − current liabilities
Non-current liabilities (due after a year): bank loan, mortgage
Net assets = non-current assets + net current assets − non-current liabilities

Financed by (total equity)
Opening capital
+ Profit for the year (or − loss)
− Drawings (money or goods the owner takes out for personal use)
= Closing capital

Net assets must equal closing capital. If they don't, something is wrong.

Adjustments
- Depreciation: add the year's charge to accumulated depreciation, so net book value falls. Straight-line: cost × %. Reducing balance: (cost − depreciation to date) × %.
- Prepayment: a current asset.
- Accrual: a current liability.

Worked example (Sam's Sports)
- NBV: equipment £20,000 − £6,000 = £14,000; van £12,000 − £4,800 = £7,200. Total £21,200.
- Current assets: £3,500 + £2,800 + £400 + £1,900 = £8,600.
- Current liabilities: £3,100 + £500 = £3,600.
- Net current assets: £8,600 − £3,600 = £5,000.
- Net assets: £21,200 + £5,000 − £8,000 loan = £18,200.
- Closing capital: £15,000 + £9,200 − £6,000 = £18,200. It balances.

Capital employed = total assets − current liabilities, or non-current liabilities + total equity. For Sam's Sports: £8,000 + £18,200 = £26,200.

Interpreting: is the business able to pay its short-term debts (net current assets positive)? How much is borrowed long-term? Is the owner taking out more in drawings than the business earns? Limitations: historic; assets at book value, not market value; doesn't value reputation or staff.

=== GLOSSARY: F2 Statement of financial position ===
- Net current assets: Current assets − current liabilities.
- Net assets: Non-current assets + net current assets − non-current liabilities.
- Closing capital: Opening capital + profit − drawings.
- Drawings: Money or goods the owner takes out for personal use.
- Current liabilities: Due within a year: overdraft, accruals, trade payables.
- Non-current liabilities: Due after a year: bank loan, mortgage.
- Capital employed: Total assets − current liabilities, or non-current liabilities + total equity.
- Purpose of the SOFP: A snapshot of assets, liabilities and capital at one date.
